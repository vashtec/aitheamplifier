AI The Amplifier

A book, published one piece at a time. New piece every week.

You have heard, sometimes from the same person, that AI has doubled, quadrupled, our engineering output, and that AI doesn't improve productivity at all. A solo founder shipping a new venture in a week, and code created by AI that no one wants to be responsible for. A beautiful demo, and a bug that never surfaces until a client starts using the thing.

Both sides of the argument are true. And neither gives a predictive framework.

Every AI use case shows a productivity gain, and everyone has a story to tell — yet macroeconomic productivity numbers haven't budged. Something else is at play.

We never question Excel when an accountant gets a report wrong, nor has a CFO ever said they didn't need an accountant anymore because an accounting template became available. So why are we doing that with AI? A machine is a machine, even if it is smarter than you. It is an amplifier. Once you see the argument as taming an amplifier, it stops being a contradiction and starts being an expectation mismatch, a trust issue, a guidance problem.

Somehow we forgot the human operating the machine.

That is what this is about.

  • The machine underneathWhy it is so confident even when it is wrong, why it bends to your view and then reinforces it, and why every task seems to eventually end in the middle ground. Tending to fall to the level of its training rather than rising to the occasion.

Why I'm writing this

I compiled my first program before I could read and write. A Commodore 128, BASIC, a manual thicker than anything I had seen before, with symbols that I could copy on the keyboard, line by line, as meticulously as you would be copying an alien drawing. All for making a song I had never heard before play, Johann Sebastian Bach's Invention No. 13.

It's all the same. Making the machine solve my master's thesis problem over a weekend, or making an AI agent do a team's work over the cloud, or a student cheating himself by cheating for an assignment. An amplifier. It's just the input that is different.

I could be wrong, on more than just a few occasions. But a chance to get something wrong is not an excuse for inaction. I have spent a working life around the amplifier, since it was a baby, and have a vantage point that felt like it might be useful to someone or two.
